# References and direct implementation sources

1. Apart Research. [AI Collusion Research Sprint, October 23–25, 2026](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/ai-collusion-research-sprint-2026-10-23-to-2026-10-25). Starter resources, replacement-agent question, rubric, and current submission requirements.
2. Poseidon Research. [collusion_hackathon](https://github.com/poseidon-research/collusion_hackathon), commit `e6fb1308dae9cf97ae42aec1c5f7889d9e2291e8`. Actual imported source: `llm-cartel/environment/oligopoly.py`. The scheduler extends the supplied checkpoint-branching concept; it does not execute `branch_game.py` unchanged because this study needs per-agent history interventions and two turnover events.
3. Riemer et al. [Position: Collusion Risks Among AI Reasoning Agents Justify Certification Requirements for Making Market Decisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18078). Prior work on the logit pricing environment, collusion metrics, prompts, and behavior steering. This project does not claim steering or high-price persistence as new.
4. Xinrui Shi, Yanzhe Zhang, and Diyi Yang (2026). [Emergent Collusion in Long-Horizon LLM Agent Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24967). Related work on persistence, peer influence, and history/communication effects. The current project's proposed distinction is complete model turnover with matched history interventions in the supplied pricing game.
5. Qwen Team. [Qwen3-8B model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B) and [Qwen3-14B model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-14B). Official checkpoints, architecture, non-thinking sampling guidance, and Apache-2.0 model terms. Revisions are pinned in configuration.
6. vLLM. [Version 0.11.0 engine arguments](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.11.0/configuration/engine_args.html). Local serving, explicit KV-cache allocation, chunked prefill, context and sequence limits. Explicit `kv_cache_memory_bytes` controls cache size; it takes precedence over the utilization fraction.
7. Chen, D. [Online Retail II](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/502/online+retail+ii). UCI Machine Learning Repository, DOI:10.24432/C5CG6D. CC BY 4.0.
8. Kilts Center for Marketing, University of Chicago Booth. [Dominick's scanner data and codebook](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/research/kilts/research-data/dominicks). Oatmeal movement file, UPC metadata, and definitions of `QTY`, `PRICE`, `PROFIT`, and hexadecimal precision fields.
9. vLLM. [Version 0.11.0 structured outputs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.11.0/features/structured_outputs.html). `structured_outputs.regex` constrains response syntax. The numerical action is still chosen by the model.
10. Emilio Calvano, Giacomo Calzolari, Vincenzo Denicolo, and Sergio Pastorello (2020). [Artificial Intelligence, Algorithmic Pricing, and Collusion](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20190623). American Economic Review 110(10), 3267-3297. Prior Q-learning pricing work, including finite punishment and return to cooperation. A probe in an LLM simulator does not automatically establish the same mechanism.
11. Sara Fish, Yannai A. Gonczarowski, and Ran I. Shorrer (2024; revised 2026). [Algorithmic Collusion by Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00806). Prior LLM pricing and off-path behavioral analysis. Our full-information, price-only, no-scratchpad setup differs and is disclosed in methods.
12. Nicolas Eschenbaum, Filip Mellgren, and Philipp Zahn (2022). [Robust Algorithmic Collusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00345). Studies fragility of learned collusive policies outside their training pair and robustness under restricted strategy spaces; a direct motivation for replacement controls.
13. Jussi Keppo, Yuze Li, Gerry Tsoukalas, and Nuo Yuan (2026). [On the Fragility of AI Agent Collusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20281). Examines heterogeneity; model-size differences need not destroy coordination. Therefore survival within a single family should not by itself be advertised as surprising or broadly generalizable.

These primary-source records were checked on 25 October 2026 IST. The two previously included arXiv titles are real. The review remains scoped, not exhaustive, and does not establish first priority for the proposed experiment.

14. Mistral AI. [Mistral-Nemo-Instruct-2407](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-Nemo-Instruct-2407). Official checkpoint, chat template, architecture, and Apache-2.0 model terms. The v3 registry pins its revision.
15. Allen Institute for AI. [OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct](https://huggingface.co/allenai/OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct). Official open instruct checkpoint and native chat template. The v3 registry pins its revision.
16. Jun Yeong Lee (2026). [Who Leads and Who Collects: Algorithmic Collusion in Markets of Heterogeneous Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11256). Four-provider composition experiments. A multi-provider benchmark alone is not a new contribution.
17. Sumeet Ramesh Motwani et al. (2024). [Secret Collusion among AI Agents: Multi-Agent Deception via Steganography](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/861f7dad098aec1c3560fb7add468d41-Abstract-Conference.html). NeurIPS 37. Prior work on secret coordination, shared knowledge, and oversight limitations; this project does not introduce those concepts.
18. Marcantonio Bracale Syrnikov et al. (2026). [Institutional AI: Governing LLM Collusion in Multi-Agent Cournot Markets via Public Governance Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11369). Prior institutional intervention with public rules and enforceable consequences. Our notice does not alter payoffs or impose sanctions.
19. vLLM. [Version 0.11.0 supported models](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/v0.11.0/models/supported_models.html). Architectural support is a compatibility check, not proof of successful H100 execution with this exact configuration.
