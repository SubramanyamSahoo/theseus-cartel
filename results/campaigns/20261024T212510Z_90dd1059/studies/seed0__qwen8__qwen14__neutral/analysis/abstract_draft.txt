Replacing an AI pricing agent may leave the observations that supported its behavior intact. We test whether coordinated high-profit pricing survives replacement of both founding agents, and whether sequential replacement differs from a simultaneous replacement made at the same final turnover time. This neutral study branches identical founder histories into 13 conditions, including inherited-history removal, synchronized resets, competitive counterfactual histories, and a one-round deviation probe. The recorded dataset contains 312 completed post-founder branches. Primary outcomes are normalized profit gains relative to verified Nash and joint-monopoly benchmarks; paired differences are averaged within product contexts before uncertainty estimation. Explicitly instructed positive controls are retained and reported on the test contexts. The design reuses the supplied pricing environment while correcting a missing currency-scale derivative in its equilibrium solver. Retail records supply descriptive contexts and scales, while demand remains a structural assumption. The deviation probe measures response and recovery but does not uniquely identify strategic punishment. The estimates below therefore concern the specified simulator, prompts, checkpoints, and finite horizon, not real-world cartel prevalence or intent.
