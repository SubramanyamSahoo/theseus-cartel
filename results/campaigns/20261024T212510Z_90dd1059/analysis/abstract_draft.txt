Does replacing every pricing agent remove a high-profit convention when successors inherit its observations? We study complete model turnover in the supplied Apart/Poseidon pricing environment, with corrected currency-scaled equilibrium benchmarks. The design includes 4 fixed checkpoints, 8 directed replacement pairs, and separate neutral and explicitly instructed founder regimes. Matched branches vary inherited histories, replacement timing, and truthful private or public replacement notices. First-action disclosure outcomes are measured before new feedback diverges. Six predeclared comparisons measure history-reset effects, public versus private first prices, and public versus administrative-placebo persistence. A one-round best-response probe is compared with copying, holding-price, and myopic-response countermodels. The recorded campaign contains 3 complete balanced seed waves; incomplete waves remain visible and are excluded from pooled primary inference. The tables report paired effects with multiplicity-adjusted intervals and context-level uncertainty. Retail observations provide descriptive contexts and scales, not identified demand or cartel labels. Disclosure sensitivity cannot establish common knowledge, and recovery after a deviation cannot uniquely identify strategic punishment. The recorded results require trace review and substantive interpretation before submission; no causal mechanism or real-market prevalence is assumed.
